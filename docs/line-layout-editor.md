# Line Layout Editor

**Read this when:** touching `src/components/LineLayoutEditor/` (dragging items onto lines, ghosts, insertion lines, right-aligning), or using it for something new.

---

## Overview

An editor for a layout of items on lines: items are tokens dragged onto a line beside other items, onto a new line, or off the lines, and right-aligned on a line or not. The items not in the layout are listed below the lines, or beside them, to drag in or tap to add. The scene info panel's editor is built on it (see [scene info panel](scene-info-panel.md) § "Customising the panel"), which supplies what the items show and their own buttons.

It's generic over the item type: the layout model and operations are in `line-layout.ts`, the geometry deciding where a dragged item goes in `drag-geometry.ts` (pure functions, unit tested), and the component in `index.tsx`. framer-motion's `Reorder` (used by the settings lists' `DraggableList`) only reorders in one direction, hence its own drag handling.

## Layout model

```ts
type Line<T> = T[] | { left: T[]; right: T[] };   // its items left to right, or its left and right-aligned ones
type Layout<T> = Line<T>[];                         // lines top to bottom
```

- A line is a plain list unless it has right-aligned items (`placeItem` stores it that way whenever it changes). Code reads a line through `lineSides` / `lineItems` rather than assuming either form.
- Positions on a line (`LayoutPosition`, `DropTarget`'s `index`) count every item on it, left then right-aligned. Between the two sides, a target's `right` decides which side it's at the end of.
- `placeItem` moves an item to a `DropTarget` (removing lines left empty, unless `keepEmptyLines`), and `replaceItem` replaces one by its key.

## Using it

| Prop | What it's for |
|---|---|
| `layout`, `onChange` | The layout, and its changes (moving, adding, removing items) |
| `getKey` | What tells an item apart from the others: its React key, `layoutId` and `data-key`. Items with the same key (which a layout's owner may keep, e.g. ones it doesn't know) get a suffix |
| `getAvailable` | The items that can be added, in their usual order, given the layout (without the dragged item while one's dragged) |
| `take` | What adding an available item puts in the layout. By default the item itself, which then leaves the available items; something else (e.g. a new instance of it) leaves it there to add again |
| `renderItem` | An item's content, given where it's rendered (`area`: on a line, among the available items, following the pointer, or for measuring), whether it's the ghost, and whether it's right-aligned |
| `renderItemActions` | An item's own button (e.g. its options), at the end of its controls (see below), on the lines and among the available items (`area` says which). `onTap` wraps a click handler so it ignores a click that isn't a tap (the one a drag ends with, or one revealing the controls) |
| `itemLabel` | What an item's called, for "Remove …" and "Add …" |
| `itemProps` | An item's extra classes and `data-` attributes |
| `toolbar`, `availableHint`, `emptyHint`, `availableBeside`, `className`, `rootAttributes` | Above the lines; the available items' hint; shown when there are no lines; the available items beside the lines rather than below; the root's class and data attributes |

- Items are Stash's tag badges (`Badge variant="secondary"` with `.tag-item`, see AGENTS.md § "File Structure Best Practices") with `.layout-item`, so their CSS only lays them out and handles dragging. An item shows only its content: its buttons are in its controls.
- **Controls:** over each item (not beside it), an overlay (`.item-controls`) in three parts: a small one at the start with a × removing it from its line (`.remove-item`), or, among the available items, a + adding it (`.add-item`, on a new line at the bottom); a drag handle (`.drag-handle`, FontAwesome's `faGripVertical`, the same as the settings lists' handles) in the middle; and a small one at the end with the item's own button, if it has one (`renderItemActions`). They show while the item's hovered over, once it's tapped with a pointer that wasn't hovering over the editor before it pressed, such as a touch (`revealed`: tapping anywhere else hides them; a press on its hidden controls reveals them rather than pressing the button under it), or while one of its buttons has keyboard focus (`:focus-visible`), and never while an item's dragged (they're hidden as a drag starts). Items are at least wide enough for them (`--item-control-width` × 3), their content centred. An item its owner makes narrower (no `min-width`) has them overflow it either side, over its neighbours (the hovered item is lifted over them, `z-index: 1`), rather than making room for them, so nothing moves; a line's first item has them overflow away from the line's edge instead (`--overflow`). Whether a pointer was hovering is whether it last moved over the editor without pressing (`hoveringPointerRef`), not its `pointerType`: a pen may hover (a drawing tablet's) or not, and ⚠️ treating every pen like a touch made a tablet's first press on the options button only reveal the controls. Hidden, they take no pointer events: otherwise overflowing controls, though invisible, took the pointer from the neighbours they lay over.
- **Where it will go:** the item following the pointer has `data-drop` (the drag target's type: `same-line`, `new-line` or `remove`) and the line it's over `.target` (with no `data-drop`, or `remove`, it has nowhere to go on the lines, and the line it was dragged off is `.from-line`, which it goes back to if let go there); once let go, the line it's sliding into is `.settle-target`. For items that look different depending on their line (e.g. Stash TV's spacers, up and down alone on a line), so the dragged copy looks as it will once it's dropped.
  - Being over the item rather than beside it, they don't make it any bigger, so the item, its ghost, the copy following the pointer and the one measured (see "Moving items") are the same size without rendering them, and don't change size as they come and go.
  - ⚠️ A touch on an item whose controls aren't showing reveals them, and doesn't press the (hidden) button under it: `startDrag` notes the press wasn't on a button, and the click that follows is ignored (`ignoreClickRef`). That's decided from the pointer's type rather than by CSS (`@media (hover: none)`), as a device with a touchscreen can also hover (and so reports `hover: hover`), and so does emulated touch in tests. Where the pointer can hover, it's over the controls, showing them, before it can click them, so hidden ones still take the pointer.
- The available items are listed below the lines, or beside them (`availableBeside`, `.side-by-side`). Dragging one up puts it where it's dropped, and its + adds it on a new line at the bottom. Dragging an item down onto that list removes it. They're listed in their usual order, and an item dragged onto them shows its ghost in its place in that order.
- Moving items has no keyboard alternative yet; adding and removing them does (they're buttons).

## Moving items

Dragging is done with pointer events (framer-motion's `Reorder`, used by the settings lists, only reorders in one direction).

**Design goal: no layout shift while dragging.** Nothing the drag shows may move the lines vertically. The item's ghost is shown in its new place only where that leaves every line exactly as tall as it is (moving items along their line is fine). Where the ghost would change a line's height (making it wrap onto another row, an item on it wrapping its text, or the line getting shorter), an insertion line marks the spot instead. A new line gets a ghost line between the lines, not a line of its own. The lines only move once the item's dropped. A layout shift doesn't just look jumpy: it moves what's under a pointer that's held still, so the target changes, the ghost moves, the layout changes back, and the ghost flips back and forth (thrashing). The rules below all follow from this goal. ⚠️ A change that lets the lines move mid-drag, with any of the items' contents (e.g. the scene info panel's pills showing names or values), breaks it. `test/e2e/scene-info-panel.test.ts` checks it with the panel's values shown, where long items wrap their text.

- Pressing anywhere on an item, its controls included, starts a drag once the pointer moves 5px. A tap (on the item, or on one of its buttons) isn't a drag.
- While dragging, the dragged item follows the pointer (`.drag-overlay`), lifted and tilted so that over its ghost, which it then hides, it doesn't look like the ghost has turned solid, and the layout is shown as it will be when it's dropped, with a faded ghost of the item in its new place (`placeItem` with a stand-in item). The ghost is the item's own item (same React key), moved and dimmed, not a separate element, without its controls. It's the same size as the item: an item changing size as it slid looked squashed.
- **Letting go:** the item doesn't jump to where it goes. The copy that followed the pointer slides there and straightens (unrotates) as it goes, and the item's element there is hidden (`.settling`) until it arrives (`settle`, measured once the drop's rendered, from where it was let go, allowing for the editor having moved, to where the element's laid out). Let go somewhere it can't go, or where it changes nothing, it slides back to the element it was dragged from. If it's no longer anywhere (an instance of a repeatable item dropped on the available items), it fades away. It slides with framer-motion's default layout transition (0.45s), so it arrives as the items moving aside for it do.
- **Where it can't go:** above or below the editor (e.g. over the video, above the scene info panel). While it's there, no ghost or insertion line is shown, and let go there it goes back. Beside the lines, past either end, is still on them, so dragging past a line's end to right-align an item works.
- Each line is a box (`.layout-line`, with `data-line`, as are its items) holding its left items and its right-aligned ones side by side, each in a box wrapping on its own (`.line-side.left` and `.line-side.right`), so the right-aligned items wrap onto rows of their own, on the right, rather than onto the left items' rows. As in the panel, the right-aligned side is laid out from the line's end (`row-reverse`), the first item at the end. The sides are at least 2em apart (`--line-sides-gap`, the line's `column-gap`, as in the panel), so they don't run together. As a flexbox does, each side is as wide as its items on one row while both fit, and otherwise both shrink in proportion to that width, no narrower than their widest item. Each side's rows line up with the other's (`align-items: flex-start`). An item (or the ghost) moving to another line or side is re-mounted there, and slides there as it keeps its `layoutId`. The space between lines is the lines' vertical margins (room for the ghost line). ⚠️ Nothing between an item and `.layout-lines` may be positioned: items are measured by their `offsetLeft`/`offsetTop`, relative to it (see "Where along the line").
- **Going on a line it would change the height of:** if showing the ghost on a line would make it taller or shorter than it is in the layout, the line stays as it is, with an insertion line (`.insertion-line`) where the item will go instead of its ghost. Only once the item's dropped does the line change. Whether it would is measured, not worked out (`probeLineHeight`): a hidden copy of the line (`.height-probe`) is laid out in the lines' container, with a copy of the dragged item as it would be on a line (`.probe-source`: rendered, unseen, as the item's pressed) in its place, and its height compared with the copy's without it (or, on the line the item was dragged off, with it where it was). That line can't get shorter while the item's dragged (see below), so on it only getting taller counts. Heights are cached for the drag, by line, side, position and the container's width. ⚠️ An earlier version worked it out from the items' widths (simulating each side wrapping), treating each item as a fixed size. That held for short items, but long ones (in the scene info panel showing values, the URLs, the file path, the details) shrink and wrap their text, so the line grew without taking another row, and the sides sharing the line in proportion to their widths could make it shorter. The ghost was shown, moved the lines after it, and thrashed. Nothing moves aside for the insertion line, so where it goes along the side of the line it's over is simply by which half of an item the pointer's over (`getSlotByMidpoints`).
- **Going on a new line:** the lines stay as they are while the item's dragged there, with a ghost line (`.ghost-line`) halfway between the lines where the new line will go, and the item's ghost leaves the line it was on. Only once it's dropped there do the lines move to make room, sliding apart. Making the new line as the item was dragged (an earlier version did) moved every line after it, and back as the item moved on. The space between lines (the lines' vertical margins) leaves room for the ghost line, and an item dragged above or below all the lines goes on a new first or last line.
- Items slide to their new places rather than jumping, whenever the layout changes in the editor: while dragging, on the drop, and when an item is added with its + or removed with its ×. They're framer-motion elements with `layout="position"` (as are the toolbar and the available items, which move as the editor grows or shrinks) (position only, as animating size stretches their text), and a `layoutId` so they also slide between lines, between a line's sides and between the lines and the available items, where they're re-mounted. ⚠️ The layoutIds are scoped to each editor (a `LayoutGroup` with an id per editor): in Stash TV every slide has its own scene info panel editor, so moving to another slide while editing had the new editor's items slide in from where the old one's had been.
- ⚠️ framer-motion only sets an element up for layout animations if it has `layout` or `layoutId` when it mounts. Turning them on later (e.g. only during a drag) leaves the items that were already there jumping to their new places.
- ⚠️ The ghost is dimmed with `filter: opacity()`, not `opacity`: framer-motion sets `opacity` inline on elements it lays out, which made the ghost flicker solid. Sharing a `layoutId` between a separate ghost and the item's item had the same effect, as framer-motion cross-fades the two (not something its version here can turn off).
- ⚠️ The drop (the new layout, ending the drag, letting go) is applied in one render (`unstable_batchedUpdates`, as it happens in a window listener React 17 doesn't batch). One render at a time, the new layout was briefly shown with the old drag's ghost and target. Items have a `layoutId`, so one moving to another line (a different parent) slides there too.
- ⚠️ The ghost has no `layoutId`, and mustn't share the dragged item's: framer-motion cross-fades elements that share one, so the ghost flickered between dim and solid as it moved. `test/e2e/scene-info-panel.test.ts` samples its opacity every frame of a drag.
- The line an item is dragged off is no shorter than it was when the drag started until the item's dropped (`min-height`, from its height then), even once it's empty (`placeItem`'s `keepEmptyLines`), so the lines after it don't move up and back. Without it, an item leaving a line that wrapped (or that it made an item wrap its text on) shrank the line.
- Targets are positions in the layout without the dragged item, with the line it was on kept even if that leaves it empty.
- **Right-aligning:** a line has items on its left and, optionally, right-aligned items, each side in its own box (see above). The right-aligned side runs from right to left, so its items, the pointer, the ghost and the pointer's sideways direction are mirrored (`mirror`, x → −x) for the rules below, which then work on it as on the left side. A new row is spotted by an item starting left of (after mirroring) *or below* the one before it (`startsRow`).
  - Which part of the line the pointer's over goes by the sides' boxes (`sideAt`). Over the empty space between them (or between one and the line's end, if the line has none of the other, or the whole line if it's empty, like the one an item was dragged off), the item goes after the left items over the space's left two thirds, and right-aligned, after the others, over its right third: they run from the line's end, so either way it's beside the space. The boxes include the ghost, which only ever makes the space smaller on its own side, so the ghost moving to one end doesn't move the pointer onto the other's part of the space.
  - Over either side, where along it goes by that side's items alone (the rules below), as each side wraps on its own. Arriving on the side the ghost isn't on works like arriving on the line.
  - Between lines, the ghost line covers the half of the lines (left or right) the pointer's over, and the new line's item is right-aligned if it's the right half.
  - The line an item's dragged onto has a faint highlight (`.layout-line.target`), as does the line the pointer's over while not dragging (`:hover`, only where the device can hover, or it stuck after a tap), so it's clear which right-aligned items share a line with which left ones. It's the line's background with a `box-shadow` spread reaching 0.25em beyond its items, with 1em corners like the items'. A pseudo-element would need the line positioned, which would change the items' `offsetParent` (see above).
- **Which line:** `getDropTarget` decides from the lines' positions: over a line, the item goes on that line. Between lines (or within 2px of a line's top or bottom edge), or above or below them all, it goes on a new line there. The edges are kept to a sliver so that a line is a bigger target than the gap beside it: with a quarter of each line's height as edges, a new line's target was about twice the size of a line's. A new line straight above or below the line an item was alone on would be where it already was, so it goes back on that line instead (`preferVacatedLine`).
- **Where along the line:** arriving on a line (`getSlotOnArrival`), the item takes the place of the item it's over, whichever part of it the pointer's over, or goes after the items to its left when it's over a gap or past the end. Taking that item's place pushes it along, often under the pointer, so that item (`arrivedOver`) doesn't count for the next rule for as long as the pointer stays over it. Leaving it across its far side puts the item after it. Leaving it any other way ends this: e.g. if it was pushed out from under the pointer (now over the ghost), coming back onto it swaps the two straight away. Once its ghost is on that line, `getSlotNearGhost` moves it past an item as soon as the pointer is over that item heading away from the ghost, so a short item passes a long one without having to reach its middle. It then stays put while the pointer carries on the same way over that item (which is now on the ghost's other side), and only moves back once the pointer turns back. Positions along the line are where the items are now, since they move aside for the ghost: where they're laid out (`offsetLeft`/`offsetTop`), not where they appear while sliding there. ⚠️ Measured mid-slide (`getBoundingClientRect`, which includes framer-motion's transform), items moved under a still pointer, so the target jumped about.
- A line too long for the editor wraps onto more rows. In the editor (not wherever the layout itself is shown), each side's later rows are indented (a hanging indent), the left side's on the left and the right-aligned side's on the right: `--wrapped-line-indent` padding on that side of the side's box, and the same negative margin on its first item, which always starts its first row (on the right as well, as it runs from the line's end). ⚠️ That item's `max-width` is 100% of the box plus the indent: the box's content area is narrower than the item by the indent, so an item alone on its line was cut short. In the editor, a marker (`.wrapped-line-marker`) in each side's indent, centred across that side's later rows, shows they're one line: ↳ on the left, and its mirror image ↲ on the right; where the rows are is down to layout, so the editor measures them after every render and when it's resized. It's keyed by its line and side and slides with the rows (`layout="position"`) when they move. Positions along each side go by the side's row the pointer's on (`rowAt`, which spots a wrap with `startsRow`). Its rows work like lines of their own: over an item on a different row from the ghost, the item goes after it if it's later in the line and before it if it's earlier, whichever way the pointer's moving sideways. Going after a later item rather than taking its place puts the item roughly where that item was, as taking the item out from before it shifts it back. Over the ghost, or on a row the ghost has to itself, nothing moves. ⚠️ Moving the ghost can move other items between rows (e.g. the ghost going after the only item on a wrapped row lets that item fit back on the row above), and taking the pointer to be on the next nearest row moved the ghost back, over and over. ⚠️ Going by the whole line instead, a pointer to the right of the last item on the *line* sent the item to the line's end, though the pointer was over the first row.
- ⚠️ Which line is worked out from where the lines were when the drag started, not where they are now. The ghost joining or leaving a line can make it wrap onto another row (or stop), moving the lines after it, so measuring them live could move the target under a pointer that was held still, and flip it back and forth.
- The toolbar, the lines and the available items are spaced by the editor's gap (`--layout-editor-gap`; a flexbox, or a grid with the available items beside the lines), so the spacing's the same either way.
- Nothing in the editor shrinks while an item's dragged. While an item is dragged, neither the lines nor the available items shrink below their height when it started (`startingHeights`): the item leaving the available items, or making a line stop wrapping, would shrink the editor, and where it grows upwards (as the scene info panel does), move everything above. They can grow (e.g. the available items taking the item's ghost), which moves the lines up, so which line the pointer's over is worked out from where the lines were relative to where their container is now.
- Pointer events are followed on the window rather than on the pressed item, which unmounts once the drag starts (it's re-rendered as the ghost).
- `placeItem` removes lines left empty.
- Items have `touch-action: none`, so dragging one on a touchscreen doesn't scroll the page (in Stash TV, the feed). Scroll the editor by touching the space around the items.
- ⚠️ A drag can end with a `click` on what was pressed (e.g. a × or a +). `ignoreClickRef` stops that click counting.
- ⚠️ Items are laid out to fractions of a pixel, and `offsetWidth`/`offsetHeight` round: measurements use `layoutSize` (`helpers/layoutOffset.ts`), which doesn't. Held at its rounded starting height, the line an item was dragged off shrank by a fraction of a pixel, moving every line after it.
- Moving items has no keyboard alternative yet; adding and removing them does (they're buttons).
